## Global Financial News

- **US President Donald Trump**: Has stated there are no immediate plans to remove Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell, despite ongoing criminal investigations, with potential future nominees excluding Treasury Secretary Steven Mnuchin.
  
- **US and China Relations**: The US Treasury warns Japan that exchange rate volatility cannot be managed solely through verbal intervention, highlighting ongoing tensions in currency markets. Meanwhile, China’s regulatory authorities are investigating Ctrip for potential monopolistic practices, which has led to a significant decline in its US-listed ADRs, reflecting increased anti-monopoly enforcement.

- **US Economic Indicators and Policy**: The US budget deficit has decreased by $109 billion (15%) in the first quarter, mainly due to higher tariff revenues, though concerns about long-term fiscal sustainability persist. The US also announced a suspension of immigrant visas to 75 countries, citing welfare dependency issues, which could impact international migration patterns.

- **Market Movements and Safe-Haven Assets**: Gold and silver prices surged to near-record highs amid geopolitical tensions, expectations of Federal Reserve rate cuts, and global uncertainties. Gold topped $4,600 per ounce, while silver surpassed $90, driven by safe-haven demand. The US dollar declined from its one-month high following the criminal probe into Fed Chair Powell, adding to market volatility.

- **Geopolitical Tensions and Regional Developments**: Iran briefly closed its airspace amid rising US-Iran tensions, leading to flight reroutes and cancellations. Iran's protests and regional instability continue to influence oil markets, with prices climbing over 3% due to supply concerns despite an unexpected build in US crude stockpiles. Iran's airspace reopened early Thursday.

- **Asia-Pacific Markets**: The region experienced mixed trading, with South Korea’s Kospi reaching a record high after the Bank of Korea kept interest rates unchanged at 2.50%. Japan’s Nikkei surged over 3% amid expectations of a snap election by the ruling Liberal Democratic Party, despite geopolitical tensions in Iran and Venezuela. Hong Kong’s Hang Seng index declined slightly, affected by regulatory concerns and sector rotations.

- **Commodity Markets**: Oil prices extended gains for a fifth day, driven by fears of Iranian supply disruptions and regional conflicts, overshadowing the US stockpile increase. Gold and silver continued their rally, reflecting investor demand for safe assets amid geopolitical unrest.

- **European Markets**: European stocks finished mixed as geopolitical tensions persisted, notably in Iran, and discussions about U.S. interest in Greenland’s Arctic future gained attention. Defense stocks rallied for a fifth consecutive day amid increased geopolitical risks, with notable gains in companies like Renk, Leonardo, and Rheinmetall.

Mainland real estate stocks led gains amid expectations of policy support and project extensions. Chinese tech stocks showed mixed performance, with some semiconductor and lithium battery companies rising on sector optimism.
